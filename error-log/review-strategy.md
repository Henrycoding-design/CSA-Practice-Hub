# How to Review Your Error Log (The Blind-List Method)

As exam day approaches, reading through your code isn't enough. You need to verify that your brain has actually "patched" the bugs in your logic. Use this **Blind-List** strategy.

## Phase 1: The Collection (Weeks Before)
Every time you get a practice problem wrong or spend more than 5 minutes debugging a single line:
1.  Open your `personal-mistake-log.md`.
2.  Fill out the template.
3.  **Crucial**: Write a clear "Reminder" sentence.

## Phase 2: The Blind Review (Days Before)
1.  Open your log and **cover the "Fix" and "Why" sections** (or just look at your "Reminder" sentences).
2.  Read the **Mistake** description.
3.  On a piece of scratch paper, try to write the **Fix** from memory.
4.  Explain the **Why** out loud (Rubber Ducking).

## Phase 3: The "Don't Panic" Scan (Morning Of)
1.  Read ONLY the **Reminder** sentences in your log.
2.  These are your personalized "rules of the road."
3.  *Example*: "If I remove from a list, I MUST decrement `i`."

## Why this works
Reading code is **passive recognition**. The Blind-List method forces **active recall**. If you can't explain the fix without looking, you haven't mastered the concept yet.
